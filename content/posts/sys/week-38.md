This week we finished our first sprint in the Scrum project. Each team had a 50-minute meeting with the Product Owner that combined the **Sprint Review** of the finished sprint with **Sprint Planning** for the next one.

The meeting was split into three parts:

```text
 0–20 min   Sprint Review
20–25 min   Adapt the Product Backlog
25–45 min   Sprint Planning
```

## Part 1: Sprint Review (0–20 min)

The review started with the **Sprint Goal**: what was the goal of the sprint, and did we reach it?

Then came the **demo**: we showed a **working increment** and explained what we had built. A demo shows real, working software. It is not a slideshow about what we *meant* to build.

The review ended with a set of questions:

- Which user stories / backlog items are **Done**?
- Are the **acceptance criteria** and the **Definition of Done** met?
- What was **not finished**?
- Has **new knowledge** come up along the way?
- Is there anything about the product that **wasn't what the PO expected**?

The last question is the most important, because it's where you find out whether you built the *right* thing and not just whether you built the thing *right*.

## Part 2: Adapting the Product Backlog (20–25 min)

The purpose of a review is to **inspect and adapt**, so right after the demo we looked at whether the review had changed anything in the Product Backlog:

- a **new user story**,
- **changed acceptance criteria**,
- something that should be **prioritized higher**,
- something that is **no longer relevant**,
- a story that should be **split**, or
- **unfinished work** that goes **back into the Product Backlog**.

That last point surprised me a bit. An unfinished story doesn't automatically carry over into the next sprint. It goes back into the Product Backlog and has to be prioritized again like everything else.

## Part 3: Sprint Planning (25–45 min)

We ran this part ourselves, with input from the PO. It started with one question:

> **What is the most important thing to achieve in the next sprint?**

Based on the Product Backlog and the PO's priorities, we formulated a **Sprint Goal** and then selected the backlog items we believed we could realistically finish.

The PO's role was to answer questions like:

- *"Which is more important, A or B?"*
- *"What do you mean by this requirement?"*
- *"Would this solution meet the need?"*

### Who decides what?

This is one of the most important principles in Scrum:

- **The PO prioritizes** *what* is most important.
- **The Developers decide** *how much* they can realistically complete.

The PO can't push more work into the sprint than the team believes it can finish. The team, in turn, doesn't decide the priorities on its own. This split keeps planning realistic and keeps the team accountable for its commitment.

## Key takeaways

- A Sprint Review is about inspecting a **working increment** against the Sprint Goal, acceptance criteria and DoD.
- The review exists to adapt the Product Backlog. New knowledge should change the plan.
- Unfinished work goes back into the Product Backlog and is prioritized again.
- In Sprint Planning, the PO decides *what* is most important and the team decides *how much* it can take.
